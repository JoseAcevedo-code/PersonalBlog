I've been meaning to do this for about three years. The blocker was never the
tooling — it was deciding what the thing was *for*. Every time I sat down to set
one up I'd end up designing a publication instead of a notebook, and then not
writing anything, because publications need an audience and a schedule and a
point of view, and notebooks just need a pen.

So this is a notebook.

## What goes in here

Mostly the middle of problems rather than the end of them. When I spend four
hours on something that should have taken twenty minutes, the useful artifact
isn't the fix — it's the map of wrong turns. That map is what I never write down
and always wish I had, usually about six months later when I hit the same
intersection.

Concretely, I expect that to look like:

- Debugging write-ups, including the ones where the bug was my own assumption
- Notes on libraries I evaluated and didn't pick, and why
- Things I got wrong in public, corrected in public

## What doesn't

Tutorials. There are enough people writing "getting started with X" and most of
them are doing it better than I would, because they've taught it to more people
than I have. If I ever write something tutorial-shaped it'll be because the
existing ones left out the part that actually confused me.

That's the whole plan. Let's see how long it survives contact with reality.
